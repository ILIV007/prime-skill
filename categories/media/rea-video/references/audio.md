# Sound breakdown

Tool outputs: the `audio` in `breakdown.json` (measured values) and `audio_plan` (the result merged with the VLM, plus the remake plan), and the `audio.png` timeline.

## How much to trust

| Item | Method | Reliability (capsule-cinema samples) |
|---|---|---|
| Voice present or not | Syllabic modulation in the voice band (3–8Hz), plus pauses between sentences | High |
| BGM present or not | Loud, pitched sound still present in voice gaps (harmonic share >0.62); without voice, harmonic persistence; or an obvious beat period | High |
| BPM | Autocorrelation of the percussive onset envelope, with half/double-speed candidates given | High (salted duck egg 110.0 vs. ground truth 110.15) |
| Key | Chroma + Krumhansl key profiles | Medium-high (both ground truths correct: D major) |
| Section changes | Novelty peaks in chroma and loudness | Medium |
| Sound effects | Very prominent transients outside voice segments and not on the beat; with BGM, they must be even more prominent | **Low**: in a voice + BGM mix, measured signal-level precision is only ~0.2; light effects are basically undetectable. The effects list follows the VLM |
| TTS or real person | Left to the VLM | — |
| Male / female voice | Median fundamental frequency (<150Hz leans male, >200Hz leans female) | Medium, reference only |
| Beat-matched editing | Share of cut points landing on beats vs. random probability | Medium-high |

## Stem separation

`decompose` by default separates the sound into three tracks under `stems/` (`rea-video stems <video>` does just this step):

| File | Content |
|---|---|
| `original.wav` | The original sound (the full mix from the video) |
| `vocals.wav` | Dry voice: narration, talking head, singing. Note: keyboard sounds and transient effects like "ding" also land here |
| `music.wav` | Score + ambient sound |

The model is UVR MDX-Net Inst HQ 3 (ONNX, CPU is fine; on first run it downloads ~67 MB from GitHub, cached in `~/.cache/rea-video/models`, relocatable via `REA_VIDEO_MODELS`). Measured SDR on a TTS + Suno BGM mix is ~19.7 dB; pure BGM leaking into the voice track is −42 dB. About 0.6× realtime (4 cores).

During review, **listen to vocals.wav and music.wav directly** — it's easier than judging from the mix: whether the voice is sung or spoken, whether it's TTS, whether the score has vocals, and whether the ending is cut off.

## Source judgment: AI-generated or stock?

**The one-line conclusion** is in `sound_sources.summary_zh`, in the format "video type → voice / music / effects sources":

| Video type | Voice | Music | Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Music MV / song | The song's own singing | **AI-generated** (Suno etc.) primarily | Effects library |
| AI video with native audio | Generated by the video model | Added score | Generated by the video model |
| Live action | Sync sound / real-person dubbing | Stock score | Sync sound |
| Narration/talking head + score | TTS or real-person dubbing | **Stock library** primarily (AI workflows also often pad with Suno) | Effects library |
| Score only | — | Stock library | Effects library |

Tool output: `sound_sources` in `breakdown.json` (for music / voice / sfx: `source`, `leaning`, `probs`, `evidence`, `how_to_confirm`).

### Step one: look at the video's form (sets the prior)

| Form | How to recognize it | Music-source prior |
|---|---|---|
| **AI music MV** (the whole piece is a song with vocals) | The VLM says format=MV; or at the deterministic layer: music fills the whole film (>85%) **and the voice is singing** (`voice.style` = singing / rap_or_rhythmic, measured on the separated dry voice) | AI-generated 60% (downweighted to 45% without a VLM) |
| **Ordinary video score** (narration/talking head + BGM underneath, or score only with no voice) | The voice is speaking (`voice.style` = speech), or there is no voice | Stock library 60% |

"Singing" vs. "speaking" is judged on the **dry voice**, with two signals:

- Sustained pitch (`sustained_note_ratio`): singing holds one pitch for ≥160ms; >0.3 counts as singing. Measured: TTS narration 0.14–0.15, rap 0.24 (rap doesn't hold long notes by nature).
- Chorus repetition (`chorus_repeat_share`): a 4-second voice-melody fragment reappears after 6 seconds. >0.1 counts as a song (rap included). Measured: AI rap MV 0.26, TTS narration 0.00. When a short video only captured one segment with no repeated chorus, this signal fails.

### Step two: correct with evidence

| Evidence | Points to | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| The lyrics sing about this film's characters/products/plot (VLM `lyrics_match_content`) | AI-generated (custom song) | Strong |
| The VLM recognizes the song title (`song_guess`) | Well-known song | Strong |
| The platform music tag is a specific song title (`--meta`) | Stock track | Strong |
| The platform tag is "original sound created by @author" | Not the platform library; MV-class pieces are mostly AI songs | Medium |
| The music is hard-cut at full volume at the end | A clipped stock track | Weak |
| Music and video end together | Custom-fit to the length / edited (either possible) | Very weak |
| The file carries an AIGC implicit marker | The whole film declares AI content | Very weak |

When the top probability is <45%, the output is "to be confirmed", with a leaning (`leaning`) given.

### Known trap (always flag it in the reply)

**Ordinary videos produced by AI workflows often have Suno-generated BGM too** (capsule-cinema's own monkey_wine, salted duck egg, and bird's nest all do). The "ordinary score = library" prior is wrong on this class of video, and by ear / signal features alone you cannot separate Suno instrumentals from library instrumentals. So conclusions for ordinary scores must be written as "leaning library, to be confirmed", with confirmation methods:

1. The music tag on the original Douyin/Xiaohongshu page (best if passed in via `--meta`).
2. Shazam-style recognition on Soda Music / QQ Music, or ACRCloud: a match means it's a stock song; no match doesn't mean it's AI (obscure library tracks often go unrecognized too).
3. By ear: AI songs have slurred diction, thin metallic highs, and lyrics tightly fitted to the picture; AI instrumentals often have an unchanging structure and an ending that stops abruptly or loops.

### Voice and effects sources

- **Voice**: TTS / AI video's native voice / real-person recording. The noise floor jumping at shot changes (`per_clip_audio.resets_at_cuts`) means each segment's sound travels with its picture clip — either an AI video model with native audio (e.g. Veo, Seedance with audio) or live-action sync sound.
- **Effects**: most come from stock effects libraries (mixkit, Aigei, etc.); when an AI video model generates audio natively, the effects travel with the clips too. The deterministic layer can only give a leaning; the list follows the VLM.

## Review method

1. Look at `audio.png`: do the voice bar, music bar, and cut lines match intuition?
2. When an effect is in doubt, extract that segment's frames to see whether there's a matching event (text pop-in, transition, action): `rea-video tiles`.
3. When the VLM gives `source_guess=popular_song`, or the platform tag is a specific song title, write "needs licensing or replacement" in the remake plan.
4. Write music sources per the "source judgment" above; never write an ordinary score as a definite "library".

## Remake

- **Voice**: capsule-cinema's DoubaoTTSTool / UniversalTTSTool or a MiniMax clone; to fit the original time window, use duration-controllable TTS.
- **BGM**: Suno (UniversalMusicGenerationTool) or a library, chosen by BPM, key, and mood; align section changes to shots.
- **Effects**: place by time point; effects with pitch are aligned to the BGM key (capsule-cinema's plush recipe sfx.py / cues.py does exactly this).
